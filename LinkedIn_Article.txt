Triangle: A New Framework for AI Source Credibility

By Martin Beyst
July 2026

---

Here's the problem nobody's solving:

AI systems aggregate information from countless sources. But they have no reliable way to know which sources are actually credible.

Search engines use popularity (PageRank).
Academia uses citation counts.
Social media uses engagement.

None of these measure what matters: who has been consistently right over time.

---

The Gap

I spent the last week searching arXiv, Wikipedia, and academic databases for work on AI source credibility.

What I found:
- 17 papers on "reputation systems" + "artificial intelligence" — none about tracking human decision quality for AI source weighting
- Wikipedia has no article on "trust in artificial intelligence"
- Wikipedia has no article on "epistemic trust"

The literature splits into two disconnected camps:

1. Reputation systems research — focused on eBay, Amazon, peer-to-peer networks
2. AI safety research — focused on alignment, guardrails, training data

Nobody is connecting them.

Nobody is saying: "Use tracked human decision quality to help AI determine which sources to trust."

---

The Idea

What if AI could weight sources not by popularity, not by credentials, but by actual track record?

What if the system knew:
- Who makes good decisions in each context
- When dissent turned out to be right
- Who is best informed in each domain

And what if that data accumulated over time — creating a reputation score based on outcomes, not attention?

---

Triangle

Triangle is a decision architecture that makes this possible.

Here's how it works:

**Three people per triangle.** Significant decisions are made by three, not one. Two agree — the decision moves. One dissents — the dissent is preserved.

**AI facilitates.** AI records inputs, tracks participation, aggregates outcomes. AI does not decide — humans decide. AI facilitates transparency.

**Authority flows to competence.** The system tracks who makes good decisions, who dissent proves right, who is best informed. Over time, authority morphs — not to title, not to tenure, but to demonstrated competence.

**At scale:** Triangles coordinate with other triangles. One triangle decides locally. Multiple triangles agree — organization-wide. The more triangles agree, the more authority a decision carries.

---

The Theological Foundation

This isn't accidental. Triangle's structure is inspired by Social Trinitarian theology — God as three persons in perfect unity, distinct yet inseparable (Moltmann, Volf, LaCugna).

This provides:
- A model of shared authority without hierarchy
- A model of distinction without division
- A model of unity without uniformity

I'm naming this honestly. The framework works for secular organizations, but its architectural inspiration is theological.

---

Application to AI

Here's where it gets interesting.

If Triangle is deployed across organizations, it accumulates a database of:
- Individual decisions and their outcomes
- Dissent records and whether dissent was retrospectively validated
- Context-specific competence
- Decision quality scores over time

This constitutes a reputation score for decision-making — based on actual tracked outcomes.

An AI system drawing on this database could weight sources by:
- Decision track record (primary signal)
- Dissent validation (sources whose dissent was right get higher weight)
- Contextual competence (domain-specific track records)

Not by popularity. Not by credentials.

By who has been consistently right.

---

Why This Is Different

Current AI: asks everyone in the crowd for advice.

Triangle-enabled AI: asks only those who have been consistently right in this domain.

This is pre-aggregation credibility scoring — not post-hoc filtering.

---

Implications

If this were applied at scale:

1. **Reduced AI hallucinations** — sources with strong track records get higher weight
2. **Surface underrepresented expertise** — strong track records without academic affiliation gain authority
3. **Preserve dissent intelligence** — minority insight isn't erased by majority vote
4. **Dynamic authority** — credibility shifts as track records evolve, preventing entrenched "expert" status

---

Limitations

- Cold start problem — requires accumulated data before scoring is meaningful
- Gaming risk — reputation systems can be manipulated (Triangle's triadic structure mitigates this)
- Domain transfer — competence in one area doesn't guarantee competence in another
- Adoption barrier — requires organizational deployment before individual data accumulates
