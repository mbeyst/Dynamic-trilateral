## Getting Started

Triangle is designed to be implemented, not just admired.

Start small. Pick one decision area. Form one triad. Track one month of decisions. Learn. Expand.

---

## Step 1: Form Your First Triad

### Who Should Be in the Triad?

**For executive decisions:**
- CEO/Executive Director
- Operations lead
- Finance/Strategy lead

**For board decisions:**
- Board chair
- One subject-matter expert
- One independent voice (not aligned with either)

**For project decisions:**
- Project lead
- Technical expert
- Stakeholder representative

### What to Avoid

- **Dyads** — Two people collapse into conflict without a tiebreaker
- **Large committees** — Five+ people diffuse accountability
- **Homogeneous triads** — Three people with identical expertise miss blind spots

### Triad Design Checklist

- [ ] Three people, distinct perspectives
- [ ] Clear decision domain (what this triad decides)
- [ ] Meeting rhythm established (weekly? per-decision?)
- [ ] Decision log created (shared, accessible)
- [ ] AI advisory access configured (if applicable)

## Step 2: Establish Decision Rules

### The 2/3 Voting Rule

1. **Unanimity is preferred** — Aim for all three in agreement
2. **2/3 proceeds** — When unanimity fails, majority rules
3. **Dissent is recorded** — The minority view is logged with the decision
4. **No pretending** — A 2-1 vote is not called "consensus"

### Dynamic Authority Protocol

Authority shifts based on:

1. **Proximity to facts** — Who is closest to the situation?
2. **Track record** — Whose prior judgments proved accurate?
3. **Vindicated dissent** — Whose past objections were proven right?

**Example:** In a safety crisis, the field coordinator leads the decision — not the CFO, not the CEO. Their dissent carries more weight.

### AI Advisory Protocol

1. **AI recommends** — Generate analysis, options, confidence levels
2. **Humans decide** — Triad votes, AI has no vote
3. **Everything logged** — AI recommendation + human vote + dissent
4. **Audit trail preserved** — Six months later, you can see who overruled the AI and why

## Step 3: Use the Decision Log

### Template

```
DECISION LOG ENTRY
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Date: [YYYY-MM-DD]
Triad Members: [Name 1], [Name 2], [Name 3]
Decision Domain: [e.g., Capital Allocation, Product Strategy]

Question:
[What decision is being made?]

AI Recommendation (if applicable):
[What did AI advise? Include confidence level if available]

Votes:
- [Name 1]: [For/Against/Abstain]
- [Name 2]: [For/Against/Abstain]
- [Name 3]: [For/Against/Abstain]

Outcome: [Approved/Rejected/Deferred]

Dissent (if any):
[Name]: [Full statement of dissenting view. Do not summarize. Preserve the actual reasoning.]

Follow-up Date: [When will this decision be reviewed?]
Follow-up Result: [Was the decision vindicated? Was dissent vindicated?]
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
```

### Best Practices

- **Record dissent verbatim** — Don't summarize. Preserve the actual reasoning.
- **Set follow-up dates** — Every decision gets reviewed in 3-6 months.
- **Track who was right** — This builds the track record for dynamic authority.
- **Make it accessible** — New triad members should read past decisions during onboarding.

## Step 4: Review and Learn

### Monthly Triad Review

Once per month, the triad reviews:

1. **Decisions made** — What did we decide?
2. **Dissent patterns** — Who dissented? Were they right?
3. **Authority shifts** — Did the right person lead each decision?
4. **AI accuracy** — When did AI recommend correctly? When did humans overrule wisely?

### Quarterly Expansion

After one quarter of successful triad operation:

1. **Document what worked** — What decision types fit the triad model?
2. **Identify gaps** — What decisions are still solo? Why?
3. **Form new triads** — Expand to adjacent decision domains
4. **Train new members** — Bring more people into the DTL framework

## Common Implementation Challenges

### "This feels slower than one person deciding"

**Response:** It is — initially. But decision *quality* improves, and implementation is faster because dissent was heard upfront.

**Mitigation:** Start with high-stakes decisions only. Don't triad every small choice.

### "Recording dissent feels uncomfortable"

**Response:** Good. That means it's working. Discomfort means you're breaking the harmony-at-all-costs pattern.

**Mitigation:** Frame dissent as "institutional memory" not "obstruction." You're preserving intelligence for future triads.

### "AI recommendations feel authoritative"

**Response:** That's the risk. AI is advisory, not decisive. The triad must consciously affirm or overrule.

**Mitigation:** Always record the AI recommendation separately from the human vote. Make the overrule explicit.

### "Who decides when authority shifts?"

**Response:** The triad decides — dynamically, per decision. This requires humility and honest track-record review.

**Mitigation:** Use the decision log. Data beats hierarchy. If someone's dissent has been vindicated three times, they lead the next related decision.
