Problems Triangle Solves

A decision architecture for leaders who believe structure matters more than charisma.

1. One person carries too much

Problem: Key decisions concentrate on one leader. They burn out, or they are wrong and nobody tells them.

Solution: Three-person triangle. One facilitator runs the process. Authority is shared by design, not by goodwill.

2. Dissent goes silent

Problem: People disagree but say nothing. The decision passes unanimously. Six months later it fails — the dissent was there but unrecorded.

Solution: Consensus-first voting. Genuine dissent is extracted, recorded, and travels with the decision. Disagreement is information, not noise.

3. Circular re-litigation

Problem: Decisions get reopened endlessly. Nothing is ever final. Teams stall because the same arguments repeat.

Solution: Lock & Forward. Once Frame → Vote → Record → Move completes, the decision is locked. The team moves to the next decision. No going back — except by challenge (two non-originating triangles) or authorized override.

4. Cross-team dependencies are invisible

Problem: Team B waits for Team A to decide. Team A does not know they are blocking. Email chains replace accountability.

Solution: Decision Requests with timeout. Team B sends a request. Team A receives it as a pending dependency on their Map. Timeout = locked. Blockers become visible. Escalation is built in.

5. No institutional memory

Problem: Why decisions were made gets lost. New members repeat old arguments. The organization learns nothing from its own history.

Solution: Every decision is recorded with reasoning, dissent, and follow-up results. The log is the Map. New members read past decisions during onboarding.

6. AI rubber-stamping

Problem: AI makes a recommendation. Humans rubber-stamp it without real evaluation. Accountability blurs.

Solution: AI is advisory only. The triangle consciously affirms or overrules. Everything is logged: AI recommendation + human vote + dissent. Six months later you can see who overruled AI and why.

7. Decisions are flat

Problem: All decisions get treated equally. Some depend on others. Some can run in parallel. Nobody can see the sequence.

Solution: Decision Map — a directed acyclic graph. Nodes are decisions. Arrows show dependencies. The Map makes the sequence visible.

8. No way to challenge bad decisions

Problem: A locked decision is wrong. One person cannot reopen it. The team is stuck.

Solution: Challenge rule. Two non-originating triangles can challenge a lock. Biblical witness pattern — by the mouth of two or three witnesses.

9. Facilitator has too much power

Problem: The person running the meeting could override the group. Minority views get suppressed.

Solution: If the facilitator is in the minority (two others disagree), the facilitator must represent the majority — not override it. When the triangle is requested to support other triangles, the majority decision binds the external position.

10. Scale breaks the rules

Problem: What works for three people does not work for thirty. Every level needs different rules.

Solution: Fractal architecture. A department is a triangle. A division is a triangle. The board is a triangle. Same rules apply at every scale. A team of five uses the same architecture as a corporation of five thousand.
